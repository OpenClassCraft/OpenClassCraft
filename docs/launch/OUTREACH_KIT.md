# Founding beta outreach kit

The goal is not maximum impressions. It is to earn conversations with Kerala teachers and school decision-makers who can run a careful Grades 5–8 pilot, then turn real classroom evidence into wider awareness.

Use an owner-controlled project identity. Configure a private official contact route before collecting personal contact details. The public GitHub application is suitable only for non-sensitive institutional information.

## Primary message

> OpenClassCraft is an offline, open-source voxel coding classroom for Grades 5–8. Students build a program as blocks inside the world, predict the result, run it through a robot, and debug it together on the school LAN.

Primary action: **Apply for the Founding School Beta.**

Public application: <https://github.com/OpenClassCraft/OpenClassCraft/issues/new?template=school_pilot.yaml>

## Who to contact first

Work outward from credibility, not audience size:

1. Kerala private schools where a principal, academic coordinator, ICT coordinator, or computer-science teacher is reachable through an official school route.
2. Teachers already running coding, robotics, STEM, makerspace, or computer clubs for Grades 5–8.
3. A small number of government/Little KITEs schools only through the appropriate official school and KITE/departmental process.
4. Kerala teacher communities, open-source groups, game-development communities, and education-technology meetups.
5. Local education, startup, technology, and game-development media after a real playable alpha and demo clip exist.

Never scrape personal contacts, add people to a bulk list without consent, or imply a government relationship. Ten researched, personal messages are more useful than a thousand generic sends.

## 30-day founder cadence

Assumption: 10–15 founder hours per week.

| Week | Proof asset | Direct work | Public work | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| Launch week | Validated Fedora alpha or honest preview demo; landing page; quickstart | 10 warm, personalised school/teacher messages | One founder story and one uncut gameplay clip | 5 replies, 2 calls |
| Week 2 | 45-minute lesson walkthrough | 10 new qualified contacts; follow up once with week-1 non-responders | “How the robot loop works” post | 2 technical checks |
| Week 3 | Teacher setup checklist and known-limitations post | 5 teacher-community introductions; 5 school messages | Malayalam quickstart excerpt and open-source post | 3 qualified applications total |
| Week 4 | First anonymised demo/pilot observation | Close technical checks and schedule pilots | What changed because a teacher tried it | 1–3 active pilots |

One respectful follow-up after five to seven working days is enough. Stop contacting a person who declines or asks not to hear more.

## Four repeatable content formats

Each public item should answer one useful question and end with one action.

1. **Predict the robot:** show a five-block sequence, ask for the final direction, then reveal the run.
2. **Teacher setup in one minute:** host, discover, join, and explain the manual-address fallback.
3. **What is still broken:** explain one honest limitation and the release gate that addresses it.
4. **Built with a teacher:** show an anonymised change from feedback, with approval and no student data.

Avoid feature-list graphics, vague “revolutionising education” language, paid follower campaigns, and screenshots of unimplemented concepts.

## Weekly scorecard

- Personal messages sent
- Replies and reply rate
- Fit calls booked and completed
- Qualified applications
- Technical checks scheduled and passed
- Pilots started
- Successful pilot sessions
- Founder support hours
- One most common objection
- One product change supported by repeated evidence

Do not optimise for followers before the qualified-call and successful-session numbers move. The strongest competitive story will be a teacher who could start a lesson, students who could explain a debug decision, and a founder who can support the school sustainably.
